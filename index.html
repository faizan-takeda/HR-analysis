Our proposed talent principles

03 / 12
Hire with evidence

A vacancy is not a reason to lower the bar.

A common selection standard; job-specific evidence. More rigor, not more interview rounds.

What we should avoid

Do not confuse similarity or pedigree with potential: subjective vetoes make decisions harder to defend.

05 / 12
Invest in existing talent

Careers should compound.

Make learning a route to harder problems, greater responsibility and new opportunities.

What we should avoid

Do not confuse course completion with skill or hold talent in one team: both limit capability transfer.

06 / 12
Performance with trust

High standards and psychological safety belong together.

Set a clear bar. Give timely feedback. Reward both results and how they are achieved.

What we should avoid

Avoid forced rankings and surprise reviews: they can discourage collaboration and early reporting of risks.

07 / 12
One enterprise, many locations

Distributed work needs shared context, not more meetings.

Make decisions accessible, connect expertise across boundaries and protect time for deep work.

61,182employees in a remote-work study; cross-group ties weakened.

What we should avoid

Do not use presence as a proxy for impact: it can disadvantage remote colleagues and conceal weak delivery.

08 / 12
Where capability becomes value

Give teams a business problem, not a list of tasks.

One accountable owner. A small, cross-functional core with experts and clear quality gates.

What we should avoid

Do not reward speed in isolation: faster delivery with weaker evidence or safety is not enterprise value.
